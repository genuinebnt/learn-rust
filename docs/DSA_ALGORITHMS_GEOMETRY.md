# Computational geometry and grid algorithms: the exhaustive target list

The target list (2026-10-10) of every computational-geometry and grid algorithm and technique that can come up on LeetCode or in an
interview, as the target for the Math & Geometry pattern lessons. The rule is in [DSA.md](DSA.md), decision 32: a topic's patterns
section is exhaustive, patterns without a NeetCode problem get **example problems** from LeetCode, and variants of one pattern
(recursive / iterative) are **tabs** of one lesson. The format follows [DSA_GRAPH_PATTERNS.md](DSA_GRAPH_PATTERNS.md); the companion
catalogue for number theory, combinatorics and discrete math is [DSA_ALGORITHMS_NUMBER_THEORY.md](DSA_ALGORITHMS_NUMBER_THEORY.md),
whose group 12 (geometry-adjacent integer problems) is the integer-only slice of this one.

Status: documented only. Today `content/dsa/lessons/math-geometry.toml` has 13 techniques (number-format, number-theory, formula,
matrix-sim, matrix-inplace, spiral, markers, number-cycle, digits, fast-pow, point-counts, median, lex-order), of which matrix-sim,
matrix-inplace, spiral and point-counts touch this list; nearly all of the list below has no lesson yet. Building the rest needs a
mockup of the tabs first, then lessons and picked example problems. Pure grid traversal (flood fill, BFS and DFS on a matrix) lives in
the Graphs topic and prefix sums on a matrix in the Arrays topic; both are cross-referenced here, not repeated.

Items written *a / b* in a group are variants for tabs where one lesson covers both (for example "Graham scan / Andrew's monotone chain").

2. **Segments and lines.** Line from two points (general form ax + by + c = 0 with integer coefficients / slope-intercept / parametric); point on a segment test (cross zero plus bounding box / dot-product range); segment intersection test (orientation cases plus collinear overlap handling / bounding box then straddle test); segment intersection point (parametric solve / line-line determinant); line-line intersection (Cramer's rule / homogeneous coordinates); parallel and perpendicular tests; distance from a point to a line (cross over length / squared form); distance from a point to a segment (clamped projection / three-case test); closest point on a segment; distance between two segments; line through two points hashing (reduced a, b, c triple / slope and intercept as fractions); reflection and mirror problems (unfolding the plane); angle bisector and perpendicular bisector; intersection of 1-D intervals (overlap / containment / gap); merging and inserting intervals as 1-D segments (sort and sweep / interval tree); interval list intersection by two pointers; removing an interval from a list of intervals; segment containment and covering (greedy by right endpoint); stabbing queries (point in how many intervals).
3. **Polygons.** Polygon representation (vertex list in order / orientation by signed area); shoelace formula (twice the area in integers / signed area for orientation); centroid of a polygon; perimeter; point in polygon (ray casting with crossing parity / winding number); point on the boundary handling; point in convex polygon in O(log n) (binary search over a fan); convexity test (all cross products of consecutive edges share a sign); simple-polygon test (no self-intersection, sweep or O(n^2)); polygon orientation correction (reverse if negative); Pick's theorem (interior lattice points from area and boundary points); lattice points on a polygon edge (gcd + 1); polygon triangulation (ear clipping / monotone polygon / trapezoidal names); area of a triangle (cross product / Heron's formula with its precision caveat); triangle inequality and valid triangle counting (sort and two pointers); triangle classification (acute / right / obtuse by squared sides); polygon validity from side lengths (largest side less than the sum of the rest); polygon clipping (Sutherland-Hodgman against a convex clip); polygon intersection area (convex / general names); Minkowski sum of convex polygons; art gallery and visibility polygons (names only); perimeter and corner counting of grid polygons (count edges not shared); regular polygon properties and counting on a cycle.

18. **Core implementation patterns.** Integer coordinates first (stay in i32 or i64, widen products to i64 or i128, never take a float for something an integer can decide); exact orientation with integer cross products;
